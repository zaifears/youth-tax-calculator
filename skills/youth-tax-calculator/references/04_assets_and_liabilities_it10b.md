5.2. Capturing assets and liabilities 
5.2.1. Description of Above Screen: 
Assets & Liabilities: 
1. Assets: An asset is a resource with a monetary value that a person, business owns or 
manages with the hope that it will bring benefits in the future . For example, Business 
capital, Land, House, Vehicle, Household items, Jewelry, any kind of Savings money 
(Sanchayapatra, FDR, DPS, Life insurance etc.), Cash in hand, Cash at bank or Card. 
 
2. Liabilities (outside business): A liability is something a person owes, usually a sum of 
money. Liabilities are settled over time through the transfer of economic benefits including 
money, goods, or services. Loan from bank or any other sum of money that you owe 
someone else is liability. If you have promised to pay someone a sum of money in the future 
and haven’t paid them yet that’s a liability. 
 
3. Other outflow: Annual Living Expense (in the expenditure IT-10BB part we have calculated 
the annual expense 2,74,264 taka which amount will be shown here automatically), Loss, 
Deduction, Gift, Donation, Contribution is the part of this section. 
 
4. Source of Fund: This is the part of Income, all the sources that you have shown income 
from, will automatically show here. Source of fund has three sections here. In this return 
the taxpayer has two sources of income, 1) taxable income (5,06,640 taka) from salary and 
2) taxable income (9,500) taka from interest on securities that is Sanchayapatra. The sum 
of 1 and 2 (5,06,640 + 9,500) = 5,16,140 is showing in Source of fund > Income Showing in 
the return section. 
 
5. Net Wealth of Previous Income Year: The value of the assets a person or corporation owns, 
minus the liabilities they owe is called net wealth, that is (Assets-Liabilities=Net Wealth). If 
the taxpayer has filed the return in the previous year, the figure that was in the ‘Net 
Wealth’ column will be placed here. If this is his first return to be submitted, then zero will 
sit in this cell. 
 
6. Summary: Whatever we earn in a financial year, we spend and save and accumulate 
wealth, i.e., Income = Expenditure + Assets. Taxpayer income on this return made 7,17,500 
taka and his expenses and assets are 7,17,500 Taka, The difference between these two is 
zero, if it is zero then your return is correct , otherwise the return should be checked 
properly where the mistake is made.

2. Non-Agricultural Property: If the taxpayer has non -agricultural property such as 
residential or commercial plot, house, apartment, etc., details of the property's 
description, value, etc. should be given in this section. For example: If someone buys a 
residential plot in installments, he has to show the a) down payment and installment 
amount before and b) total installment amount for this year. 
 
3. Agricultural Property: If the taxpayer owns agricultural property, then he has to show in 
this section details, such as - Description and Location, whether the property was 
purchased by him or inherited or received as a gift and the amount of land etc. 
 
4. Financial Asset: Financial assets of the taxpayer such as Shares, Debentures, Savings 
certificates (Sanchayapatra), Bonds and other securities, Fixed Deposits (FDR), Savings 
Pension Schemes (DPS), Lending (Loan given to others) and any other financial assets 
should be given in this section. 
 
5. Motor Car: If the taxpayer has a private motor vehicle - Jeep or Microbus, its value, 
registration, and incidental expenses should be entered in this section. If there is more 
than one vehicle, then the sum of each should be given. 
 
6. Gold, Diamond, Gems and Other Items: If the taxpayer has any ornaments including Gold, 
Diamonds, Gems or precious stones then the details should be mentioned here. In the 
case of gifts, since the value is not known, the value can be given as zero. 
 
7. Furniture, Equipment and Electronic Items: Furniture (Beds, Tables, Chairs, Sofa etc.) 
Equipment (Refrigerator, washing machine, cooking stove etc.), Electronic (TV, 
Computer, Laptop, Mobile etc.) should be recorded in this section. 
 
8. Other Assets of Significant Value: In addition, if the taxpayer has any other valuable 
property other than the property mentioned in serial number 1 to 7, its information 
should be provided in this serial. 
 
9. Cash and Fund outside Business: Cash, bank, card or electronic money balances, 
provident fund balances and other deposits, balances or advances other than those 
mentioned in column 4 should be shown in this section.
