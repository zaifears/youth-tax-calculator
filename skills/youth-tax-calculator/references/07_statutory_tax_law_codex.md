# Bangladesh Income Tax Act 2023 (ITA 2023) Statutory Codex
### Comprehensive Legal Reference for Youth, Students, Interns & Young Professionals
*Governing Law: Income Tax Act 2023 (Act No. XII of 2023 / আয়কর আইন, ২০২৩) & Prevailing Finance Acts*

---

## Part I: Constitutional & Statutory Foundations

### 1.1 The Legal Framework
Under Article 83 of the Constitution of the People's Republic of Bangladesh, no tax shall be levied or collected except by or under the authority of an Act of Parliament.
Individual income taxation in Bangladesh is governed by:
1. **The Income Tax Act 2023 (ITA 2023):** Enacted to replace the historic *Income Tax Ordinance 1984 (ITO 1984)*. It standardizes modern accounting, digital filing, and restructured income heads into 345 Bengali-language sections.
2. **The Annual Finance Act (অর্থ আইন):** Enacted on or before 30 June each year to amend tax slabs, tax-free exemption ceilings, surcharge thresholds, and rebate rates for the corresponding Assessment Year (effective 1 July).
3. **Statutory Regulatory Orders (S.R.O.s):** Subordinate legislation issued by the National Board of Revenue (NBR) with Ministry of Law vetting under powers conferred by Section 343 of ITA 2023. S.R.O.s carry binding statutory authority unless ultra vires the parent Act.
4. **Official Circulars and General Orders (পরিপত্র):** Administrative instructions issued by the NBR clarifying ambiguities in statutory interpretation.

### 1.2 Income Year vs. Assessment Year (Section 2)
* **Income Year (আয় বর্ষ - Section 2(9)):** The 12-month financial period from **1 July to 30 June** during which income is accrued, earned, or received.
* **Assessment Year (কর বর্ষ - Section 2(22)):** The 12-month period immediately following the income year (commencing 1 July) during which the income earned in the preceding income year is assessed to tax.
  * *Example:* Income earned between **1 July 2025 and 30 June 2026** is assessed in **Assessment Year 2026–2027**.

## Part II: Classification of Income (Section 30)

Income is legally classified into seven mutually exclusive heads under Section 30 of ITA 2023:

```
Section 30: Heads of Income (আয়ের খাতসমূহ)
├── Section 32: Income from Employment (চাকরি হইতে আয়)
├── Section 35: Income from Financial Assets (আর্থিক পরিসম্পদ হইতে আয়)
├── Section 38: Income from Other Sources (অন্যান্য উৎস হইতে আয়)
├── Section 40: Income from Rent (ভাড়া হইতে আয়)
├── Section 45: Income from Agriculture (কৃষি হইতে আয়)
├── Section 49: Income from Business (ব্যবসা হইতে আয়)
└── Section 57: Capital Gains (মূলধনী আয়)
```

### 2.1 Income from Employment (Section 32 / Schedule 1)
* **Scope:** All remuneration, wages, basic salary, overtime, bonuses, honoraria, and perquisites received by an individual in connection with an employment or contractual engagement.
* **Internship Wages & Stipends:** Contracted internship stipends paid by corporate entities, consultancies, or institutions constitute "Employment Income" under Section 32, as an employer-employee or contractual service relationship exists.
* **Statutory Salary Exemption (Sixth Schedule, Part 1, Paragraph 1):**
  $$\text{Exempt Employment Amount} = \min\left(\frac{1}{3} \times \text{Gross Salary}, \text{ BDT } 4,50,000\right)$$
  * The remaining balance after deducting this statutory one-third allowance is the **Net Taxable Employment Income**.
  * *Practical Case:* If a student earns BDT 35,000 from an internship, one-third ($\text{BDT } 11,667$) is automatically exempt by law, leaving only $\text{BDT } 23,333$ taxable.

### 2.2 Income from Financial Assets (Section 35)
* **Substantive Reform:** ITA 2023 abolished the separate head "Interest on Securities" and consolidated all investment and banking yields into Section 35:
  1. **Bank Interest / Profit:** Savings accounts, term deposits, and fixed deposits.
  2. **Stock Dividends:** Cash dividends distributed by listed or private companies.
  3. **Islamic P2P Financing & Crowdfunding:** Profit markups generated through licensed Islamic micro-crowdfunding platforms.
  4. **Bonds & Debentures:** Yields from approved corporate bonds or sovereign Sukuk.
* **Withholding Tax (TDS Credit - Section 133 & 153):**
  * Banks deduct 10% TDS (with TIN) or 15% TDS (without TIN) on interest under Section 102.
  * Companies deduct 10% TDS (with TIN) or 15% TDS (without TIN) on dividends under Section 117.
  * **Taxpayer Right:** All source tax deducted is creditable against final tax liability. If total tax payable is BDT 0.00, the full TDS amount is statutorily refundable under Section 160.

### 2.3 Capital Gains (Section 57)
* **Scope:** Any profits or gains arising from the transfer of a capital asset.
* **Secondary Market Listed Stocks:** Realized gains from secondary market stock trades held in Beneficiary Owner (BO) accounts are calculated as:
  $$\text{Realized Capital Gain} = \text{Gross Sale Proceeds} - (\text{Cost of Acquisition} + \text{Brokerage Commissions})$$
* **Statutory Tax Rates:** Capital gains from listed shares of public companies for general individual residents are taxed at reduced statutory rates or subject to specific annual Finance Act exemptions.

### 2.4 Income from Other Sources (Section 38)
* **Residual Nature:** Covers any income not strictly classifiable under Sections 32 through 57.
* **Student Competitions & Academic Prize Winnings:**
  * Prize money won in university hackathons, business case competitions, or innovation challenges falls under Section 38.
  * **The Beneficial Ownership Principle (Section 2(31)):** When prize money clears into a team leader's account on behalf of a multi-member team, only the leader's **individual proportionate share** (e.g., 1/4th) constitutes their taxable income under Section 38. The outward disbursement of the teammates' shares does not constitute a personal expense or gift; it is a fiduciary pass-through transfer.

## Part III: Exemptions & Statutory Rebates (Sixth Schedule)

### 3.1 Fully Exempt Incomes (Sixth Schedule, Part 1)
Income listed in Part 1 of the Sixth Schedule is completely excluded from the computation of total taxable income under Section 26(1):

* **Paragraph 8: Student Stipends & Scholarships:**
  > *"Any scholarship, stipend or grant granted to a person to meet the cost of education."*
  * **Legal Significance:** 100% tax-free. Whether granted by a public university, private university, foreign government, or institutional grant, no tax can be levied on this money.
  * **Wealth Effect:** While excluded from taxable income, it **is fully recognized as an accretive inflow of capital**. It must be declared in the return to lawfully justify bank balances and living expenses without being flagged as unexplained wealth.

* **Paragraph 21: IT-Enabled Services (ITeS) / Software:**
  * Income derived from nationwide software development, cloud services, and IT-enabled services under prevailing S.R.O. conditions.

* **Paragraph 24: Foreign Remittances:**
  * Remittances received through formal banking channels from non-resident Bangladeshis (NRBs) or overseas relatives.

### 3.2 Investment Tax Rebates (Sixth Schedule, Part 2)
Under Section 78, taxpayers are entitled to an investment tax rebate deducted directly from their Gross Tax Liability:

$$\text{Rebate} = \min \begin{cases} 
15\% \times \text{Eligible Investment} \\ 
3\% \times \text{Total Taxable Income} \\ 
\text{BDT } 10,00,000 \text{ (Statutory Absolute Cap)} 
\end{cases}$$

#### Statutory Eligible Investment Avenues:
1. SEC-approved Open-End and Closed-End Mutual Funds (held at cost).
2. Deposit Pension Scheme (DPS) in any scheduled bank or financial institution (annual deposit allowable up to BDT 1,20,000).
3. Primary and secondary market purchases of shares/debentures listed on Dhaka Stock Exchange (DSE) or Chittagong Stock Exchange (CSE).
4. Life and health insurance premiums (allowable up to 10% of the policy sum assured).
5. Government Zakat Fund or government-approved philanthropic trusts.

## Part IV: Multi-Broker Loss Set-off & Carry-Forward (Sections 70 & 72)

Young investors trading across multiple brokerage houses face complex loss set-off rules:

### 4.1 Intra-Head Capital Loss Set-Off (Section 70(1))
* Where in respect of any assessment year, the net result of the computation under the head "Capital Gains" is a loss, such loss can be set off against capital gains arising from any other capital asset in the same income year.
* *Example:* A gain of BDT 896 in Brokerage A is offset against a loss of BDT 3,924 in Brokerage B, resulting in an unabsorbed net loss of BDT 3,028.

### 4.2 The Ring-Fence Rule (Section 70(2))
* Capital losses are legally ring-fenced: **a capital loss cannot be set off against income under any other head** (e.g., salary, bank interest, or consulting). It cannot make the taxpayer's total income negative.

### 4.3 6-Year Carry-Forward Mechanism (Section 70(3))
* Any unabsorbed net capital loss:
  1. Is carried forward to the following assessment year.
  2. Can be set off **only against capital gains** in that subsequent year.
  3. Can be carried forward for a maximum of **six (6) consecutive assessment years** immediately succeeding the year in which the loss was first computed.

### 4.4 Legal Analysis of the Section 70 e-Return PDF Discrepancy
* **The Portal Artifact:** In NBR's automated 14-page IT-11GA draft return, the backend calculation script deducts carried-forward capital losses from total receipts in the summary reconciliation box.
* **The Result:** Page 9 Line 7 (Gross Wealth) shows an amount lower than Page 10 Line 10 (Total Assets) by the exact carried-forward capital loss:
  $$\text{Line 7 (Gross Wealth)} + \text{Section 70 Loss} = \text{Line 10 (Total Assets)}$$
* **The Judicial Defense:** Under Section 70, the carried loss is not an expenditure or asset destruction; it is a memorandum tax attribute. The discrepancy is a documented system reporting limitation of Synesis IT's PDF generator, fully justified and reconciled by the official *Schedule of Loss Carry Forward* appended on Page 12 of the return. It does NOT constitute a reporting concealment under Section 212.

## Part V: High Bank Turnover & The Deemed Income Trap (Section 56)

### 5.1 The Danger of Section 56 (Unexplained Wealth)
Under Section 56, the Deputy Commissioner of Taxes (DCT) is legally empowered to deem unexplained deposits, investments, or assets as undisclosed taxable income:
* **Section 56(a): Unexplained Cash Credits:** Any sum credited in the books/bank account of the taxpayer without a verifiable source.
* **Section 56(b): Unexplained Investments:** Assets acquired without corresponding declared income or non-taxable receipts.
* **Consequences:** Taxed at maximum marginal rates (up to 30%), plus concealment penalties up to 100% of the tax evaded (Section 212).

### 5.2 The Parental Support Shield (Section 56(g))
* **The Statute:** Section 56(g) explicitly excludes gifts and living support received from specific blood relatives—namely **parents (father/mother), spouse, and children**—from the definition of deemed income.
* **Classification:** Money received from parents is a **non-taxable capital receipt**.
* **Proper Reporting:** It must not be entered as taxable income; rather, it is declared under **Form IT-10B (Statement of Assets & Liabilities) $\rightarrow$ Source of Fund $\rightarrow$ Other Receipts (Manual Page 60)**.
* **Audit Immunity:** Even if a student has BDT 20–30 Lakh flowing through their bank account, if the incoming funds represent documented parental transfers (`ABBU`, `MOM`, father's bank account) or educational maintenance, Section 56(g) provides complete statutory audit immunity.

## Part VI: The Balance Sheet Law (IT-10B & IT-10BB)

### 6.1 Statutory Criteria for Filing Form IT-10B (Section 166)
Filing the Statement of Assets, Liabilities and Life Style (Form IT-10B) is legally mandatory if the taxpayer:
1. Possesses gross assets exceeding **BDT 50,00,000 (50 Lakh)**; OR
2. Owns a motor vehicle (car, jeep, microbus); OR
3. Owns a house property or apartment in a City Corporation area; OR
4. Holds shares or a directorship in a limited company; OR
5. Incurred international travel expenses.

* **Voluntary Submission for Youth:** If a student or intern has wealth under BDT 50 Lakh, filing IT-10B is voluntary. However, **voluntary submission is strongly recommended** because it creates an unbroken, legal baseline of wealth. Future asset accumulations (buying land, vehicles, or mutual funds) can then be justified by past savings without triggering audit scrutiny.

### 6.2 The Zero-Difference Statutory Balance Sheet Equation
Under the NBR eReturn System User Manual (Pages 61–62) and standard accounting practice under ITA 2023, the balance sheet must balance to zero:

$$\text{Difference} = \text{Total Fund Outflow} - \text{Total Source of Fund} \equiv \mathbf{0.00}$$

Where:
1. $\Delta W = \text{Closing Net Wealth (Line 3)} - \text{Opening Net Wealth (Line 4)}$
2. $\text{Total Fund Outflow} = \Delta W + \text{Form IT-10BB Living Expenses} + \text{Other Outflows (Gifts Given)}$
3. $\text{Total Source of Fund} = \text{Taxable Income} + \text{Exempt Stipends} + \text{Other Receipts}$
4. **The Balancing Parental Support Equation:**
   $$\text{Declared Parental Support} = \text{Total Fund Outflow} - (\text{Taxable Income} + \text{Exempt Stipends})$$

### 6.3 Asset Valuation Rules
* **Financial Assets:** Declared at **historic acquisition cost**, NOT current market valuation or Net Asset Value (NAV).
* **Gifted or Inherited Gold (Manual Page 51):** Declared in weight (Bhori/Grams). If acquired as a gift or inheritance with unknown historical cost, NBR official rules explicitly permit entering the monetary value as **`0`**.
* **Personal Effects:** Used laptops, smartphones, and clothing under BDT 50,000 bought in prior years need not be declared as new asset additions, avoiding artificial cash outflow distortion.

## Part VII: Universal Self-Assessment & Audit Defense (Sections 180, 182, 183)

### 7.1 Universal Self-Assessment (Section 180)
All online returns submitted via `etaxnbr.gov.bd` are legally processed under **Universal Self-Assessment (সার্বজনীন স্বনির্ধারণী পদ্ধতি - Section 180)**:
* The acknowledgement receipt generated by the system is deemed to be an assessment order passed by the DCT on the date of submission.
* The taxpayer is immediately issued the **Proof of Submission of Return (PSR)** required under Section 264 for opening bank accounts, taking loans, obtaining credit cards, and 43+ civic services.

### 7.2 Selection for Audit (Section 182)
The NBR Central Board may select a return for audit using automated risk parameters (e.g., mismatch between banking data and declared income). If selected:
* The DCT must issue a formal written notice specifying the inquiries.
* The DCT **cannot arbitrarily reject** documented parental support under Section 56(g) or statutory salary exemptions under Section 32.
* The taxpayer has the right to present their indexed dossier.

### 7.3 Statutory Audit Defense Dossier (6-Year Retention Rule)
Under Sections 182 and 183, the statute of limitations allows audits within six (6) years of submission. The taxpayer must preserve:
1. **Full-year bank statements** (annotated for family transfers and team disbursements).
2. **Bank and dividend withholding certificates** showing interest, fees, and TDS deducted.
3. **Signed broker portfolio statements** showing 30 June share holdings at cost.
4. **Mutual fund and P2P tax certificates** documenting cost and profits.
5. **Academic fee clearance records** and university ID (Sixth Schedule, Part 1, Para 8).
6. **Parental confirmation letter:** A simple signed declaration from father/mother stating that they provided living and educational maintenance during the income year under Section 56(g).
7. **Official PSR & Submitted Form IT-11GA** downloaded from `etaxnbr.gov.bd`.
